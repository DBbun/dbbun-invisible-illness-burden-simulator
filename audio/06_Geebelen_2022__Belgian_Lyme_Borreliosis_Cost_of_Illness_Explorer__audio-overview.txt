This audio overview explains the D B bun Invisible Illness Burden Simulator. It was developed by Dr. Uri Kartoun, founder of D B bun L L C, in Cambridge, Massachusetts. The example here is the simulator titled: Belgian Lyme Borreliosis Cost-of-Illness Explorer (a cited study twenty twenty-two).

This is a peer-reviewed Belgian cost-of-illness study that estimates the yearly economic burden of Lyme borreliosis using a prospective patient cohort (HUMTICK) and national hospital registries.

What it covers, and what it does not: This simulation reproduces the per-patient and national annual cost figures reported by a Belgian prospective cohort and hospital-registry cost-of-illness study of erythema migrans (early Lyme rash) versus disseminated or late Lyme borreliosis (including post-treatment Lyme disease syndrome, post-treatment Lyme disease syndrome).

First, what is the value? Every simulator is written for three kinds of users at once, and the results page can be switched between the three views.

For patients and advocates: Using this Belgian study's own reported figures, a patient diagnosed with disseminated or late-stage Lyme disease had a typical total cost (medical care, travel, and lost income combined) several times higher than a patient diagnosed with the early rash stage (erythema migrans), and about a quarter of direct medical costs were not reimbursed by insurance in this cohort.

For care delivery teams: This scenario shows that per-patient costs for disseminated or late-stage Lyme borreliosis far exceed those for early-stage erythema migrans in this Belgian cohort, and that Belgium's hospital coding system cannot separately capture erythema migrans or post-treatment Lyme disease syndrome hospital presentations - both findings are relevant to how early-detection pathways and diagnosis coding are structured.

For payers and policy audiences: This scenario reproduces the study's national annual cost estimate for Lyme borreliosis in Belgium by combining each disease-stage subgroup's own case count and per-patient cost, showing that disseminated or late-stage disease drives the majority of national cost despite being far less common than early-stage disease, and quantifies the reimbursed-versus-patient-paid cost split relevant to coverage policy.

For all three, the adjustable model lets them ask what changes if an assumption changes.

Why build it this way? A published paper gives you its numbers frozen in one year and one place. You cannot change an assumption, compare the figures with national data, or see what the paper never measured. In the simulator, the document becomes runnable, not only readable. For example, in the scenario named More disseminated in the mix: Annual erythema migrans case count from eleven thousand one hundred sixty-eight cases per year to ten thousand six hundred fifty-seven cases per year and Annual disseminated or late Lyme borreliosis case count from six hundred seventy-three cases per year to one thousand one hundred eighty-four cases per year. Then Total annual national Lyme borreliosis cost moves from five million six hundred twenty thousand twenty-eight euros a year to eight million one hundred fifty-two thousand thirty-three euros a year. Every result updates at once, and nothing is re-typed or recalculated by hand.

How does it work? A small local text classifier decides which condition a document is about, using keyword matching and a method called term frequency, inverse document frequency. That is the machine learning step, and it is not a large language model. It selects eligible federal evidence from a pre-built library of about fourteen hundred and fifty-four units, where a unit is one federal data file, such as a table, a spreadsheet or a guideline document, pre-processed into a short text summary. For this document, three hundred seventy-one units were eligible and ten were picked as short excerpts, from four from Centers for Disease Control and Prevention Lyme disease surveillance, two from International Classification of Diseases, Tenth Revision, Clinical Modification coding guidelines, two from Bureau of Labor Statistics labor cost data, two from Bureau of Economic Analysis regional economics data. Then generative artificial intelligence, Claude Sonnet, is called four times to extract the findings, draft the interactive simulator, write the scenarios, and write downloadable Python code. Computer code, not the artificial intelligence, does the arithmetic and the federal checks. Every number is labeled observed, assumed or projected.

How were the federal resources used for this simulator? The numbers come from the document. Federal data decided which sources were eligible, was offered to the model as background, and feeds the computed checks. One quantity from the document was read for checking; zero were compared with federal data and one could not be compared. Total annual number of clinically diagnosed Lyme borreliosis cases (erythema migrans + disseminated or late Lyme borreliosis) used for national cost extrapolation: not computed. The geography "Belgium (national)" is neither the United States nor a United States state. The model cited one of the ten retrieved units as evidence, and the simulator records that.

Now the what-if scenarios. The simulator opens at the document's reported values. At those defaults, Total annual national Lyme borreliosis cost is five million six hundred twenty thousand twenty-eight euros a year; Disseminated or late Lyme borreliosis minus erythema migrans per-patient cost is four thousand nine hundred fifty-five euros; Disseminated or late Lyme borreliosis cost divided by erythema migrans cost is twenty-six point seven; Direct medical cost share not reimbursed by insurance is twenty-four point five percent.

There are three named scenarios, one click each. More disseminated in the mix sets Annual erythema migrans case count to ten thousand six hundred fifty-seven cases per year and Annual disseminated or late Lyme borreliosis case count to one thousand one hundred eighty-four cases per year, so Total annual national Lyme borreliosis cost becomes eight million one hundred fifty-two thousand thirty-three euros a year. Fewer disseminated in the mix sets Annual erythema migrans case count to eleven thousand four hundred eighty-six cases per year and Annual disseminated or late Lyme borreliosis case count to three hundred fifty-five cases per year, so Total annual national Lyme borreliosis cost becomes four million forty-four thousand three hundred thirty-eight euros a year. Atypically low tick-bite year sets Annual erythema migrans case count to seven thousand cases per year and Annual disseminated or late Lyme borreliosis case count to four hundred cases per year, so Total annual national Lyme borreliosis cost becomes three million four hundred ten thousand two hundred euros a year. These scenario values are illustrative bounds chosen for the simulator, not findings from the source. They show how far a result moves, not what will happen.

This simulator has no forecast chart. It also uses Monte Carlo simulation. The Monte Carlo step draws five hundred random combinations of the assumptions, each within its own range. For Total annual national Lyme borreliosis cost, those draws run from nine hundred thirty-six thousand three hundred ninety-four euros a year to twenty-six million thirty-three thousand nine hundred sixty-seven euros a year, with a mean of nine million fifty-eight thousand four hundred forty-five euros a year. That spread reflects only the assumed exploration ranges of the inputs. It is not a probability or a confidence interval. The draws and their summary are included in the downloads, as files named monte carlo sample and monte carlo summary.

What does this document not measure? The simulator sorts results into sixteen burden types. This document has results for one of them: direct costs of care. The other fifteen are empty here. They include work and income, caregiving and household labor, cognitive impact, psychological burden, and overall quality of life. An empty type is a gap in what this simulator covers, not proof that the burden is absent, and the document may discuss some of them without reporting a result.

The limits are these. Retrieval is keyword-based and runs once per document, so the best match for a specific figure can be missed. Only three kinds of figures are cross-checked in code. Federal data shown to the model is not the same as federal data used. The burden categories are assigned by an artificial intelligence model. And the simulator has not been validated as a forecast.

The potential: in principle, the same approach could be applied to any published paper focused on Lyme disease or Long COVID, which could be transformative. That is a potential, not yet shown beyond those two conditions. This simulator is part of D B bun's infrastructure, and the concept could be extended to any disease, with a vision of wide use, for example by PubMed, the National Library of Medicine, and libraries. That is a vision.

You can take the results with you: a nine-sheet Excel workbook, comma-separated files and Python code, which statistical software and languages such as R can read, and there is a cross-pollination analysis that sets Lyme disease and Long COVID simulators side by side. In the full tool, an Application Programming Interface and an Administrator page for adding federal resources are also available.
