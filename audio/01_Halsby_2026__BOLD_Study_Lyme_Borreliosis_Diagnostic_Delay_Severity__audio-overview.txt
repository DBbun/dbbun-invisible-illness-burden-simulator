This audio overview explains the D B bun Invisible Illness Burden Simulator. It was developed by Dr. Uri Kartoun, founder of D B bun L L C, in Cambridge, Massachusetts. The example here is the simulator titled: BOLD Study: Lyme Borreliosis Diagnostic Delay, Severity Mix, and Antibiotic Duration (six European Countries, twenty twenty-one to twenty twenty-two).

This is the BOLD (Burden of Lyme Disease) study, a prospective, descriptive surveillance study run at fourteen primary-care practices in Lyme-endemic areas of the Czech Republic, Germany, Poland, Slovakia, Slovenia, and Sweden between April twenty twenty-one and December twenty twenty-two.

What it covers, and what it does not: This simulator reproduces only the clinical and treatment-pattern measures the BOLD study reports for medically attended Lyme borreliosis cases in the Czech Republic, Germany, Poland, Slovakia, Slovenia, and Sweden (April twenty twenty-one-December twenty twenty-two): time to diagnosis by presentation type, hospitalization rate, disseminated-disease proportion, and country-level antibiotic treatment duration.

First, what is the value? Every simulator is written for three kinds of users at once, and the results page can be switched between the three views.

For patients and advocates: In this European study of three hundred fifteen enrolled Lyme disease patients (twenty twenty-one to twenty twenty-two), the average time from first symptoms to diagnosis was about thirteen days when the only sign was the classic rash (erythema migrans), compared with about twenty-five days when the infection had already spread beyond the skin (disseminated disease, such as joint, nerve, or heart involvement) - roughly an eleven-day gap.

For care delivery teams: Across six European primary-care networks (twenty twenty-one to twenty twenty-two), cases presenting with disseminated disease (spread beyond the inoculation site - for example, Lyme arthritis, neuroborreliosis, carditis) took about twenty-five days on average from symptom onset to diagnosis, versus about thirteen days for erythema-migrans-only cases, roughly an eleven-day gap.

For payers and policy audiences: In this six-country European surveillance study (BOLD, twenty twenty-one to twenty twenty-two), twenty-two point eight percent of seven hundred ninety-seven medically attended Lyme borreliosis cases had disseminated disease, with large country-to-country variation reported (as low as one point one percent in Slovenia and as high as seventy-eight percent in Slovakia).

For all three, the adjustable model lets them ask what changes if an assumption changes.

Why build it this way? A published paper gives you its numbers frozen in one year and one place. You cannot change an assumption, compare the figures with national data, or see what the paper never measured. In the simulator, the document becomes runnable, not only readable. For example, in the scenario named Slovakia's disseminated-disease share: Disseminated-disease proportion, all medically attended cases from twenty-two point eight percent to seventy-eight percent. Then Disseminated-disease proportion, all medically attended cases moves from twenty-two point eight percent to seventy-eight percent. Every result updates at once, and nothing is re-typed or recalculated by hand.

How does it work? A small local text classifier decides which condition a document is about, using keyword matching and a method called term frequency, inverse document frequency. That is the machine learning step, and it is not a large language model. It selects eligible federal evidence from a pre-built library of about fourteen hundred and fifty-four units, where a unit is one federal data file, such as a table, a spreadsheet or a guideline document, pre-processed into a short text summary. For this document, three hundred seventy-one units were eligible and ten were picked as short excerpts, from four from Centers for Disease Control and Prevention Lyme disease surveillance, two from Centers for Disease Control and Prevention tick surveillance, two from International Classification of Diseases, Tenth Revision, Clinical Modification coding guidelines, two from Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services Lyme disease cost benchmarks. Then generative artificial intelligence, Claude Sonnet, is called four times to extract the findings, draft the interactive simulator, write the scenarios, and write downloadable Python code. Computer code, not the artificial intelligence, does the arithmetic and the federal checks. Every number is labeled observed, assumed or projected.

How were the federal resources used for this simulator? The numbers come from the document. Federal data decided which sources were eligible, was offered to the model as background, and feeds the computed checks. No federal check applied to this document. The model did not cite any of the ten retrieved units as evidence, and the simulator records that too.

Now the what-if scenarios. The simulator opens at the document's reported values. At those defaults, Diagnostic delay gap, disseminated minus erythema migrans-only is eleven point five days; Antibiotic duration gap, Poland minus Slovakia is fourteen point five days; Hospitalization rate, enrolled cases is one point three percent; Disseminated-disease proportion, all medically attended cases is twenty-two point eight percent.

There are three named scenarios, one click each. Slovakia's disseminated-disease share sets Disseminated-disease proportion, all medically attended cases to seventy-eight percent, so Disseminated-disease proportion, all medically attended cases becomes seventy-eight percent. Slovenia's disseminated-disease share sets Disseminated-disease proportion, all medically attended cases to one point one percent, so Disseminated-disease proportion, all medically attended cases becomes one point one percent. European Union overall median versus Poland sets Median antibiotic treatment duration, Slovakia to eleven days, so Antibiotic duration gap, Poland minus Slovakia becomes twelve point five days. These scenario values are illustrative bounds chosen for the simulator, not findings from the source. They show how far a result moves, not what will happen.

This simulator has no forecast chart. It also uses Monte Carlo simulation. The Monte Carlo step draws five hundred random combinations of the assumptions, each within its own range. For Diagnostic delay gap, disseminated minus erythema migrans-only, those draws run from one point five days to twenty-eight point two days, with a mean of fifteen point seven days. That spread reflects only the assumed exploration ranges of the inputs. It is not a probability or a confidence interval. The draws and their summary are included in the downloads, as files named monte carlo sample and monte carlo summary.

What does this document not measure? The simulator sorts results into sixteen burden types. This document has results for one of them: diagnostic delay. The other fifteen are empty here. They include work and income, caregiving and household labor, cognitive impact, psychological burden, and overall quality of life. An empty type is a gap in what this simulator covers, not proof that the burden is absent, and the document may discuss some of them without reporting a result.

The limits are these. Retrieval is keyword-based and runs once per document, so the best match for a specific figure can be missed. Only three kinds of figures are cross-checked in code. Federal data shown to the model is not the same as federal data used. The burden categories are assigned by an artificial intelligence model. And the simulator has not been validated as a forecast.

The potential: in principle, the same approach could be applied to any published paper focused on Lyme disease or Long COVID, which could be transformative. That is a potential, not yet shown beyond those two conditions. This simulator is part of D B bun's infrastructure, and the concept could be extended to any disease, with a vision of wide use, for example by PubMed, the National Library of Medicine, and libraries. That is a vision.

You can take the results with you: a nine-sheet Excel workbook, comma-separated files and Python code, which statistical software and languages such as R can read, and there is a cross-pollination analysis that sets Lyme disease and Long COVID simulators side by side. In the full tool, an Application Programming Interface and an Administrator page for adding federal resources are also available.
