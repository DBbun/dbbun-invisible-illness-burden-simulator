This audio overview explains one cross-pollination analysis from the D B bun Invisible Illness Burden Simulator, developed by Dr. Uri Kartoun, founder of D B bun L L C, in Cambridge, Massachusetts. A cross-pollination analysis sets one Lyme disease simulator beside one Long COVID simulator and asks what each condition's researchers could learn from the other. Each simulator is built from one published paper, and the analysis compares what the two papers report. It does not describe either condition in general.

The two simulators compared here are, first, BOLD Study: Lyme Borreliosis Diagnostic Delay, Severity Mix, and Antibiotic Duration, on the Lyme disease side, and second, twenty twenty-two National Health Interview Survey Long COVID Prevalence Explorer, on the Long COVID side.

The summary says: This comparison covers two papers, one built from a European Lyme disease surveillance study and one built from a United States national Long COVID prevalence survey, and it does not describe either condition in general. The Lyme disease paper offers diagnostic-delay, hospitalization, and treatment-duration figures but no cost, insurance, or income-loss data, while the Long COVID paper offers national and demographic prevalence figures but no delay, severity, or cost data. The one shared pattern that can be shown with real figures is that both source documents are silent on direct and indirect costs, access barriers, and productivity loss. Because there is exactly one Lyme disease paper and one Long COVID paper, and no paper covering both conditions, every row above reflects what these two specific documents happen to report rather than a broader claim about either condition.

The headline findings are these. This comparison covers two papers, one Lyme disease paper and one Long COVID paper, not a general comparison of the two conditions. The Lyme disease paper reports diagnostic-delay and hospitalization figures but no cost, insurance, or income-loss data, while the Long COVID paper reports national and demographic prevalence figures but no cost, delay, or severity data either. Neither source document contains any direct medical cost, productivity-loss, or caregiving-time figures, so no financial-burden comparison between the two conditions is possible from this evidence.

The Long COVID paper contains no diagnostic-delay measure; it reports only self-reported ever or current prevalence. What the two papers share: Neither source document behind these two papers provides any cost, insurance-access, or productivity-loss figures, leaving a complete financial-burden gap for both conditions in this comparison. What neither paper provides: Neither source document behind these two papers includes any direct medical cost, indirect cost, or caregiving-time data, so no cost-of-illness comparison can be built from this evidence alone.

The comparison also lists questions worth asking, one of them here. Severity may delay diagnosis; worth checking for Long COVID too. The question worth asking, from the Lyme paper to Long COVID: Could Long COVID researchers check whether people with more severe or multi-system presentations are diagnosed later than those with milder, single-symptom presentations?

How was it checked? The numbers are copied from the two simulators. The wording is written by an artificial intelligence model and checked in code, and any number the code cannot match is flagged. In this comparison, no numbers were flagged. The analysis used three model calls. It is also placed on a chart among all twenty-five simulators, and a list shows which other simulators fill the gaps the pair leaves.

The limits: This is a small, non-random sample built from whichever documents happened to be uploaded: one Lyme disease paper and one Long COVID paper, with no paper covering both conditions, as given in the real figures. It is not a controlled study, and agreement or disagreement between these two papers does not validate either one; findings reflect only what each specific source document reports. The two papers differ in population, year and method, so the comparison gives context and questions. It does not measure a difference between the diseases.

The potential: in principle, the same approach could be applied to any published paper focused on Lyme disease or Long COVID, which could be transformative. That is a potential, not yet shown beyond those two conditions.
