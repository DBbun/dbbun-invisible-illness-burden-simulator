This audio overview explains the D B bun Invisible Illness Burden Simulator. It was developed by Dr. Uri Kartoun, founder of D B bun L L C, in Cambridge, Massachusetts. The example here is the simulator titled: Long COVID Incidence and Risk-Factor Explorer (RECOVER electronic health records Networks Study).

This is a peer-reviewed epidemiological study, not a cost study, that used electronic health record (electronic health records) data from three large United States research networks to estimate how often people who had COVID nineteen went on to develop Long COVID, using several different computable 'definitions' (algorithms) of the condition.

What it covers, and what it does not: This simulator represents only incidence proportions, background-adjusted ('excess') incidence estimates, and sex-based risk-factor hazard ratios for Long COVID reported from three United States electronic health record (electronic health records) research networks (NthreeC, PCORnet, PEDSnet), measured within one hundred eighty days (or two hundred seventy days for a harmonized definition) of a documented COVID nineteen infection, twenty twenty to twenty twenty-three.

First, what is the value? Every simulator is written for three kinds of users at once, and the results page can be switched between the three views.

For patients and advocates: This study looked at electronic health records from three large research networks and found that, among people who tested positive for COVID nineteen, somewhere between about ten percent and twenty-six percent of adults (and about four percent of children) were later coded with Long COVID within six months, depending on which network and definition was used.

For care delivery teams: Across three electronic health records networks, reported Long COVID incidence proportions among COVID nineteen-positive patients ranged widely (about ten percent to twenty-six percent in adults within one hundred eighty days; about four percent in children), reflecting different computable phenotypes (a rules-based International Classification of Diseases, Tenth Revision approach versus a machine-learning approach) applied to different, only partially overlapping populations - not a single agreed-upon case definition.

For payers and policy audiences: Three electronic health records networks produced adult Long COVID incidence estimates ranging from about ten percent to twenty-six percent of COVID nineteen-positive adults within one hundred eighty days, and about four percent in children, using structurally different computable phenotypes and populations that the authors say should not be pooled into one national figure.

For all three, the adjustable model lets them ask what changes if an assumption changes.

Why build it this way? A published paper gives you its numbers frozen in one year and one place. You cannot change an assumption, compare the figures with national data, or see what the paper never measured. In the simulator, the document becomes runnable, not only readable. For example, in the scenario named Harmonized two hundred seventy-day definition: NthreeC adult crude incidence from ten point two percent to twelve point six percent and PCORnet adult crude incidence from twenty-five point nine percent to nine point six percent. Then PCORnet minus NthreeC adult crude incidence moves from fifteen point seven percentage points to minus three percentage points. Every result updates at once, and nothing is re-typed or recalculated by hand.

How does it work? A small local text classifier decides which condition a document is about, using keyword matching and a method called term frequency, inverse document frequency. That is the machine learning step, and it is not a large language model. It selects eligible federal evidence from a pre-built library of about fourteen hundred and fifty-four units, where a unit is one federal data file, such as a table, a spreadsheet or a guideline document, pre-processed into a short text summary. For this document, six hundred forty-six units were eligible and ten were picked as short excerpts, from eight from International Classification of Diseases, Tenth Revision, Clinical Modification coding guidelines, one from Centers for Disease Control and Prevention Long COVID aggregate data for twenty twenty-two through twenty twenty-four, one from Bureau of Economic Analysis regional economics data. Then generative artificial intelligence, Claude Sonnet, is called five times to extract the findings, draft the interactive simulator, write the scenarios, and write downloadable Python code. Computer code, not the artificial intelligence, does the arithmetic and the federal checks. Every number is labeled observed, assumed or projected.

How were the federal resources used for this simulator? The numbers come from the document. Federal data decided which sources were eligible, was offered to the model as background, and feeds the computed checks. No federal check applied to this document. The model did not cite any of the ten retrieved units as evidence, and the simulator records that too.

Now the what-if scenarios. The simulator opens at the document's reported values. At those defaults, PCORnet minus NthreeC adult crude incidence is fifteen point seven percentage points; NthreeC adult excess incidence attributable to COVID nineteen is five point three percentage points; PCORnet adult excess incidence attributable to COVID nineteen is six point three percentage points; PEDSnet child excess incidence attributable to COVID nineteen is one point four percentage points.

There are three named scenarios, one click each. Harmonized two hundred seventy-day definition sets NthreeC adult crude incidence to twelve point six percent and PCORnet adult crude incidence to nine point six percent, so PCORnet minus NthreeC adult crude incidence becomes minus three percentage points. Pediatric female-risk value in NthreeC sets NthreeC adjusted hazard ratio, female versus male to one point two hazard ratio, so NthreeC minus PCORnet adjusted hazard ratio for female sex becomes zero hazard ratio difference. PCORnet matches NthreeC's female-risk estimate sets PCORnet adjusted hazard ratio, female versus male to one point four hazard ratio, so NthreeC minus PCORnet adjusted hazard ratio for female sex becomes zero hazard ratio difference. These scenario values are illustrative bounds chosen for the simulator, not findings from the source. They show how far a result moves, not what will happen.

This simulator has no forecast chart. It also uses Monte Carlo simulation. The Monte Carlo step draws five hundred random combinations of the assumptions, each within its own range. For PCORnet minus NthreeC adult crude incidence, those draws run from minus forty-four point seven percentage points to forty-eight point three percentage points, with a mean of minus zero point four percentage points. That spread reflects only the assumed exploration ranges of the inputs. It is not a probability or a confidence interval. The draws and their summary are included in the downloads, as files named monte carlo sample and monte carlo summary.

What does this document not measure? The simulator sorts results into sixteen burden types. This document has results for one of them: undercounted by official surveillance. The other fifteen are empty here. They include work and income, caregiving and household labor, cognitive impact, psychological burden, and overall quality of life. An empty type is a gap in what this simulator covers, not proof that the burden is absent, and the document may discuss some of them without reporting a result.

The limits are these. Retrieval is keyword-based and runs once per document, so the best match for a specific figure can be missed. Only three kinds of figures are cross-checked in code. Federal data shown to the model is not the same as federal data used. The burden categories are assigned by an artificial intelligence model. And the simulator has not been validated as a forecast.

The potential: in principle, the same approach could be applied to any published paper focused on Lyme disease or Long COVID, which could be transformative. That is a potential, not yet shown beyond those two conditions. This simulator is part of D B bun's infrastructure, and the concept could be extended to any disease, with a vision of wide use, for example by PubMed, the National Library of Medicine, and libraries. That is a vision.

You can take the results with you: a nine-sheet Excel workbook, comma-separated files and Python code, which statistical software and languages such as R can read, and there is a cross-pollination analysis that sets Lyme disease and Long COVID simulators side by side. In the full tool, an Application Programming Interface and an Administrator page for adding federal resources are also available.
