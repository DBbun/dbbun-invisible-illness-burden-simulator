This audio overview explains the D B bun Invisible Illness Burden Simulator. It was developed by Doctor Uri Kartoon, founder of D B bun L L C, in Cambridge, Massachusetts. The example here is the simulator titled: Long COVID Care-Coordination and Symptom-Duration Explorer (Single-Clinic Qualitative Study, n=twenty-one).

This is a qualitative study in which researchers interviewed twenty-one adult patients at an academic medical center's post-COVID recovery clinic about their experiences seeking and receiving care for long COVID.

What it covers, and what it does not: This simulation represents only what a qualitative interview study of twenty-one adult patients at one academic post-COVID recovery clinic reported: a stated range for how long severe symptoms lasted, the clinic's minimum eligibility duration, and the specialist types patients described being referred across.

First, what is the value? Every simulator is written for three kinds of users at once, and the results page can be switched between the three views.

For patients and advocates: In this study of twenty-one adults at one academic post-COVID recovery clinic, patients reported symptoms lasting one to two years, well beyond the three-month minimum needed to qualify for the clinic's care, and many described confusion over which specialist to tell about new or worsening symptoms.

For care delivery teams: Among the twenty-one patients interviewed at this academic post-COVID recovery clinic, reported symptom duration (one to two years) consistently exceeded the clinic's three-month eligibility threshold, and patients frequently described confusion about which specialist to update as they moved across referrals to cardiology, physical therapy, rehabilitation psychology, and speech therapy.

For payers and policy audiences: This qualitative interview study of twenty-one patients at one academic post-COVID recovery clinic reported symptom duration (one to two years) that exceeded the clinic's three-month eligibility threshold, and documented a systemic gap in long COVID care: the authors describe a shortage of providers knowledgeable about the condition and a lack of evidence-based management guidelines, with specialists addressing symptoms within their own domain without a coordinated pathway across them.

For all three, the adjustable model lets them ask what changes if an assumption changes.

Why build it this way? A published paper gives you its numbers frozen in one year and one place. You cannot change an assumption, compare the figures with national data, or see what the paper never measured. In the simulator, the document becomes runnable, not only readable. For example, in the scenario named Shorter illustrative duration range: Lower bound of reported symptom duration from twelve months to six months and Upper bound of reported symptom duration from twenty-four months to eighteen months. Then Lower-bound symptom duration minus clinic eligibility threshold moves from nine months to three months. Every result updates at once, and nothing is re-typed or recalculated by hand.

How does it work? A small local text classifier decides which condition a document is about, using keyword matching and a method called term frequency, inverse document frequency. That is the machine learning step, and it is not a large language model. It selects eligible federal evidence from a pre-built library of about fourteen hundred and fifty-four units, where a unit is one federal data file, such as a table, a spreadsheet or a guideline document, pre-processed into a short text summary. For this document, four hundred fifty-one units were eligible and ten were picked as short excerpts, from eight from Household Pulse Survey Long COVID data from the United States Census Bureau, one from International Classification of Diseases, Tenth Revision, Clinical Modification coding guidelines, one from Centers for Disease Control and Prevention Long COVID aggregate data for twenty twenty-two through twenty twenty-four. Then generative A I, Claude Sonnet, is called four times to extract the findings, draft the interactive simulator, write the scenarios, and write downloadable Python code, at a total cost here of about fifty-one cents. Computer code, not the A I, does the arithmetic and the federal checks. Every number is labeled observed, assumed or projected.

How were the federal resources used for this simulator? The numbers come from the document. Federal data decided which sources were eligible, was offered to the model as background, and feeds the computed checks. No federal check applied to this document. The model did not cite any of the ten retrieved units as evidence, and the simulator records that too.

Now the what-if scenarios. The simulator opens at the document's reported values. At those defaults, Reported symptom-duration range width is twelve months; Lower-bound symptom duration minus clinic eligibility threshold is nine months; Illustrative count of potential communication links among reported specialist types is six links.

There are three named scenarios, one click each. Shorter illustrative duration range sets Lower bound of reported symptom duration to six months and Upper bound of reported symptom duration to eighteen months, so Lower-bound symptom duration minus clinic eligibility threshold becomes three months. Longer illustrative duration range sets Lower bound of reported symptom duration to six months and Upper bound of reported symptom duration to thirty months, so Reported symptom-duration range width becomes twenty-four months; Lower-bound symptom duration minus clinic eligibility threshold becomes three months. Stricter clinic eligibility policy sets Clinic minimum symptom-duration eligibility to six months, so Lower-bound symptom duration minus clinic eligibility threshold becomes six months. These scenario values are illustrative bounds chosen for the simulator, not findings from the source. They show how far a result moves, not what will happen.

This simulator has no forecast chart. It also uses Monte Carlo simulation. The Monte Carlo step draws five hundred random combinations of the assumptions, each within its own range. For Reported symptom-duration range width, those draws run from zero point eight months to twenty-two point eight months, with a mean of eleven point six months. That spread reflects only the assumed exploration ranges of the inputs. It is not a probability or a confidence interval. The draws and their summary are included in the downloads, as files named monte carlo sample and monte carlo summary.

What does this document not measure? The simulator sorts results into sixteen burden types. This document has results for one of them: uncoordinated, fragmented care. The other fifteen are empty here. They include work and income, caregiving and household labor, cognitive impact, psychological burden, and overall quality of life. An empty type is a gap in what this simulator covers, not proof that the burden is absent, and the document may discuss some of them without reporting a result.

The limits are these. Retrieval is keyword-based and runs once per document, so the best match for a specific figure can be missed. Only three kinds of figures are cross-checked in code. Federal data shown to the model is not the same as federal data used. The burden categories are assigned by an A I model. And the simulator has not been validated as a forecast.

The potential: in principle, the same approach could be applied to any published paper focused on Lyme disease or Long COVID, which could be transformative. That is a potential, not yet shown beyond those two conditions. This simulator is part of D B bun's infrastructure, and the concept could be extended to any disease, with a vision of wide use, for example by PubMed, the National Library of Medicine, and libraries. That is a vision, not something tested.

You can take the results with you: a nine-sheet Excel workbook, comma-separated files and Python code, which statistical software and languages such as R can read, and there is a cross-pollination analysis that sets Lyme disease and Long COVID simulators side by side. In the full tool, an Application Programming Interface and an Administrator page for adding federal resources are also available.

Once more: this is an educational what-if model, not medical advice. It is an independent entry, not endorsed by the Department of Health and Human Services or the National Institutes of Health.
