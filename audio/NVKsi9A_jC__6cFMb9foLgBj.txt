This audio overview explains the D B bun Invisible Illness Burden Simulator. It was developed by Doctor Uri Kartoon, founder of D B bun L L C, in Cambridge, Massachusetts. The example here is the simulator titled: Long COVID Symptom Patterns and Self-Reported Work or Economic Impact (Japanese thirty-six-Month Cohort).

This is a Japanese multicenter retrospective cohort study that followed five hundred fifteen adults hospitalized with COVID nineteen (first to third pandemic waves, Jan twenty twenty-Feb twenty twenty-one) for thirty-six months using questionnaires.

What it covers, and what it does not: This simulator reflects only a five hundred fifteen-adult Japanese multicenter cohort of patients hospitalized for COVID nineteen (pandemic waves one-three January twenty twenty-February twenty twenty-one), followed by self-report questionnaire to thirty-six months (survey conducted November twenty twenty-three-February twenty twenty-four).

First, what is the value? Every simulator is written for three kinds of users at once, and the results page can be switched between the three views.

For patients and advocates: In this Japanese study of five hundred fifteen adults hospitalized with COVID nineteen and followed for three years, people whose long COVID symptoms continued the whole time, or who had several symptoms at once, were more likely to report that their work performance was affected and that their overall economic situation had gotten worse, compared with people whose symptoms went away.

For care delivery teams: Among the five hundred fifteen adults hospitalized with COVID nineteen in this Japanese cohort followed to thirty-six months, patients in the 'continuous' symptom trajectory or the 'multiple symptoms' cluster (identified at three months) had the worst self-reported work-performance (presenteeism) scores and the highest rates of self-reported economic worsening (about forty-four percent in the continuous group versus about fifteen percent in the recovered group, a roughly twenty-nine-percentage-point gap, p<zero across trajectory groups).

For payers and policy audiences: In this five hundred fifteen-patient Japanese multicenter cohort of adults hospitalized with COVID nineteen (waves one-three January twenty twenty-February twenty twenty-one) followed to thirty-six months, twenty-five point six percent (one hundred thirty-two or five hundred fifteen) met the study's long COVID definition at three years.

For all three, the adjustable model lets them ask what changes if an assumption changes.

Why build it this way? A published paper gives you its numbers frozen in one year and one place. You cannot change an assumption, compare the figures with national data, or see what the paper never measured. In the simulator, the document becomes runnable, not only readable. For example, in the scenario named Narrower economic-worsening gap: Economic condition worsened, continuous-trajectory group from forty-three point six percent to thirty-five percent and Economic condition worsened, recovered-trajectory group from fourteen point nine percent to twenty-five percent. Then Economic worsening gap: continuous minus recovered trajectory group moves from twenty-eight point seven percentage points to ten percentage points. Every result updates at once, and nothing is re-typed or recalculated by hand.

How does it work? A small local text classifier decides which condition a document is about, using keyword matching and a method called term frequency, inverse document frequency. That is the machine learning step, and it is not a large language model. It selects eligible federal evidence from a pre-built library of about fourteen hundred and fifty-four units, where a unit is one federal data file, such as a table, a spreadsheet or a guideline document, pre-processed into a short text summary. For this document, four hundred thirty-eight units were eligible and ten were picked as short excerpts, from seven from National Health Expenditure Accounts from the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services, one from International Classification of Diseases, Tenth Revision, Clinical Modification coding guidelines, one from Centers for Disease Control and Prevention Long COVID aggregate data for twenty twenty-two through twenty twenty-four, one from Bureau of Economic Analysis regional economics data. Then generative A I, Claude Sonnet, is called four times to extract the findings, draft the interactive simulator, write the scenarios, and write downloadable Python code, at a total cost here of about seventy-seven cents. Computer code, not the A I, does the arithmetic and the federal checks. Every number is labeled observed, assumed or projected.

How were the federal resources used for this simulator? The numbers come from the document. Federal data decided which sources were eligible, was offered to the model as background, and feeds the computed checks. No federal check applied to this document. The model did not cite any of the ten retrieved units as evidence, and the simulator records that too.

Now the what-if scenarios. The simulator opens at the document's reported values. At those defaults, Economic worsening gap: continuous minus recovered trajectory group is twenty-eight point seven percentage points; Economic worsening gap: five-or-more symptoms minus one-to-four symptoms is twenty-three percentage points; Presenteeism score, continuous-trajectory group is sixty World Health Organization Health and Work Performance Questionnaire score (zero-one hundred scale, study's own definition); Presenteeism score, 'multiple symptoms' cluster is forty-eight point eight World Health Organization Health and Work Performance Questionnaire score (zero-one hundred scale, study's own definition).

There are three named scenarios, one click each. Narrower economic-worsening gap sets Economic condition worsened, continuous-trajectory group to thirty-five percent and Economic condition worsened, recovered-trajectory group to twenty-five percent, so Economic worsening gap: continuous minus recovered trajectory group becomes ten percentage points. Wider economic-worsening gap sets Economic condition worsened, continuous-trajectory group to fifty-five percent and Economic condition worsened, recovered-trajectory group to five percent, so Economic worsening gap: continuous minus recovered trajectory group becomes fifty percentage points. Closed symptom-count gap sets Economic condition worsened, five-or-more-symptom group to thirty-five percent and Economic condition worsened, one-to-four-symptom group to thirty-five percent, so Economic worsening gap: five-or-more symptoms minus one-to-four symptoms becomes zero percentage points. These scenario values are illustrative bounds chosen for the simulator, not findings from the source. They show how far a result moves, not what will happen.

This simulator has no forecast chart. It also uses Monte Carlo simulation. The Monte Carlo step draws five hundred random combinations of the assumptions, each within its own range. For Economic worsening gap: continuous minus recovered trajectory group, those draws run from twelve point five percentage points to forty-nine point three percentage points, with a mean of twenty-nine point seven percentage points. That spread reflects only the assumed exploration ranges of the inputs. It is not a probability or a confidence interval. The draws and their summary are included in the downloads, as files named monte carlo sample and monte carlo summary.

What does this document not measure? The simulator sorts results into sixteen burden types. This document has results for one of them: work and income. The other fifteen are empty here. They include caregiving and household labor, cognitive impact, psychological burden, overall quality of life, and direct costs of care. An empty type is a gap in what this simulator covers, not proof that the burden is absent, and the document may discuss some of them without reporting a result.

The limits are these. Retrieval is keyword-based and runs once per document, so the best match for a specific figure can be missed. Only three kinds of figures are cross-checked in code. Federal data shown to the model is not the same as federal data used. The burden categories are assigned by an A I model. And the simulator has not been validated as a forecast.

The potential: in principle, the same approach could be applied to any published paper focused on Lyme disease or Long COVID, which could be transformative. That is a potential, not yet shown beyond those two conditions. This simulator is part of D B bun's infrastructure, and the concept could be extended to any disease, with a vision of wide use, for example by PubMed, the National Library of Medicine, and libraries. That is a vision, not something tested.

You can take the results with you: a nine-sheet Excel workbook, comma-separated files and Python code, which statistical software and languages such as R can read, and there is a cross-pollination analysis that sets Lyme disease and Long COVID simulators side by side. In the full tool, an Application Programming Interface and an Administrator page for adding federal resources are also available.
