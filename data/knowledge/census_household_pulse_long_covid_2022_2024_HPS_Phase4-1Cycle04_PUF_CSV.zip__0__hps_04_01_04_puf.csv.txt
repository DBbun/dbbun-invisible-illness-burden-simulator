RELEASE-SPECIFIC FINDING
HPS_Phase4-1Cycle04_PUF_CSV.zip contains 73,352 respondent records; 3,575 meet the current-case coding rule. The weighted proportion is 4.2767%, with a design-based approximate 95% interval 4.0320% to 4.5213%. This is measured prevalence, not the fraction whose work loss was caused by Long COVID.

ECONOMIC DECISION VALUE
This release can establish the population scale and observed functional-impact mix for a support-planning scenario. It cannot supply a wage, recoverable work hours, program uptake or intervention effectiveness. Do not multiply this prevalence by a national published total cost as though the latter were per person. Activity limitation supports assessment of accessibility needs; it is not equivalent to employment exit or eligibility for benefits.

DEFINITION AND MISSINGNESS
LONGCOVID yes codes are [1, 2, 3]. Phase 4.1 expanded yes into duration categories; previous binary codes cannot be reused unchanged. Negative response codes and skipped items are retained in the all-adult denominator, not reclassified as observed no. Functional impact is measured separately among current cases with valid impact responses.

DECISIONS BY AUDIENCE
Patients and caregivers: Use prevalence to contextualize experiences, not to predict your wages or recovery. Functional impact is a separate question from having symptoms.
Care delivery: Plan accessible follow-up using the functional-impact categories where present. Collect work hours and care needs separately.
Payers and policy: Use this release-specific denominator and uncertainty for population planning; obtain independent evidence for costs and effects before claiming savings.

MISSING EVIDENCE / NEXT ANALYSIS
No identified causal effect of workplace support or earlier recognition.
Work-loss attribution, wage valuation and unpaid-care hours are not estimated here.
Missing-item denominator sensitivity and alternative survey definitions should be reviewed before model promotion.

DO NOT USE FOR
Pooling releases as independent unique people, interpreting a prevalence change as recovery, or inferring personal disability from population proportions.
Adding this microdata estimate and the CDC HPS dashboard estimate as independent populations.

Analysis is source-backed but has not had independent scientific review. No modeled savings or assumed effects are supplied.
